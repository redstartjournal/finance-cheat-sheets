# Finance Cheat Sheets

Money and investing explained in pictures, one idea per sheet. Made in Europe, euro first, in plain English.

Each sheet shows how something really works: where your fees go, how an ETF is built, what happens after you click
"buy". New sheets arrive every day, one category at a time. Every fact on a sheet has a source, listed under it.

The animated versions, and lessons that go deeper, are free at [Red Start Education](https://education.redstartjournal.com/cheat-sheets).

> Education, not investment advice. No sheet tells you what to buy or sell.

## How to use them

- Read them, save them, print them, share them, use them in class. Please keep the credit to Red Start Journal.

### Money Basics

- [Where Your Pay Goes](sheets/money-basics/where-your-pay-goes.md): Gross pay minus income tax and social contributions is your net pay, the money that reaches your account. One example, no country's rates.
- [The 50/30/20 Split](sheets/money-basics/the-50-30-20-split.md): One rule of thumb to split pay: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% for your future. For example, on €2,000 net pay. It is not the only way.
- [How Big an Emergency Fund?](sheets/money-basics/how-big-an-emergency-fund.md): A common range is 3 to 6 months of your costs, in cash you can reach fast. For example €4,500 to €9,000 on €1,500 a month.
- [Net Worth in One Picture](sheets/money-basics/net-worth-in-one-picture.md): Net worth is what you own minus what you owe. For example €25,000 owned minus €10,000 owed is €15,000.
- [Where Your Savings Account Money Goes](sheets/money-basics/where-your-savings-account-money-goes.md): Your bank keeps a small part at the central bank, uses the rest for loans, and pays you part of what the loans earn. Example rates.
- [Is Your Bank Money Safe?](sheets/money-basics/is-your-bank-money-safe.md): In the EU, deposits are guaranteed up to €100,000 per person, per bank, paid back in 7 working days. Shares and funds are not deposits.
- [Saving vs Investing](sheets/money-basics/saving-vs-investing.md): Saving is for money you need soon and want steady. Investing is for later, and its value goes up and down.
- [Simple vs Compound Interest](sheets/money-basics/simple-vs-compound-interest.md): Simple interest is paid on your money only. Compound interest is paid on your interest too. For example €1,000 at 5% for 10 years.
- [How Compound Interest Works](sheets/money-basics/how-compound-interest-works.md): Each year's interest earns interest too. For example €1,000 at 5% a year grows to about €1,600 in 10 years and €4,300 in 30.
- [The Rule of 72](sheets/money-basics/the-rule-of-72.md): Divide 72 by the yearly rate to estimate the years to double your money. At 6% that is about 12 years. It is an estimate.
- [What Inflation Does to €1,000](sheets/money-basics/what-inflation-does-to-1000-euros.md): At 2% inflation a year, €1,000 kept for 10 years buys what about €820 buys today. 2% is the ECB's target for the euro area.
- [Nominal vs Real Returns](sheets/money-basics/nominal-vs-real-returns.md): Nominal return is the growth on paper. Real return is that growth minus rising prices: 5% growth with 2% inflation is about 3% real.
- [Smaller Payments, Bigger Bill](sheets/money-basics/smaller-payments-bigger-bill.md): The same €1,000 at 20% a year costs about €1,110 over 1 year and €1,590 over 5. The smallest monthly payment costs the most.
- [How a Credit Card Charges You](sheets/money-basics/how-a-credit-card-charges-you.md): Pay the card bill in full and interest is €0. Pay only part and interest builds on what you still owe.
- [The Real Cost of a Loan (APR)](sheets/money-basics/the-real-cost-of-a-loan-apr.md): The APR is one yearly percentage that includes interest and fees. EU lenders must show it before you sign.
- [Fixed vs Variable Rate](sheets/money-basics/fixed-vs-variable-rate.md): A fixed rate keeps every payment the same; a variable rate moves with the market. For example €377 a month can become €528.
- [Buy Now, Pay Later](sheets/money-basics/buy-now-pay-later.md): Buy now, pay later splits a price into small parts that feel free until one is late. EU rules treat it as credit from 20 November 2026.

### Fees & Costs

- [Where Your €1,000 Goes](sheets/fees-and-costs/where-your-1000-euros-goes.md): Buying a US share from a European account: the fees your €1,000 meets on the way, step by step.
- [Top 6 Fees That Eat Returns](sheets/fees-and-costs/top-6-fees-that-eat-returns.md): Six costs that quietly shrink what you keep: fund fees, commissions, currency exchange, the spread, account fees and trade taxes.
- [What 1% a Year Costs Over 30 Years](sheets/fees-and-costs/what-1-a-year-costs-over-30-years.md): The same €10,000 over 30 years, for example at 6% a year with no cost and 5% after a 1% cost: about €57,000 against €43,000.
- [FX Fees: The Silent One](sheets/fees-and-costs/fx-fees-the-silent-one.md): A US share bought from a euro account changes currency on the way in, on the way out and on every dividend.
- [The Spread Is a Fee Too](sheets/fees-and-costs/the-spread-is-a-fee-too.md): Every share has a buy price and a sell price. The gap is a cost you pay, and it is never on your bill.
- [How Brokers Make Money](sheets/fees-and-costs/how-brokers-make-money.md): The nine ways a retail broker earns, who pays each one, and why "zero commission" is not zero cost.
- [Custody and Inactivity Fees](sheets/fees-and-costs/custody-and-inactivity-fees.md): Six costs of just holding investments: custody, account, inactivity, transfer-out, small extras and taxes on holdings.
- [Entry and Exit Charges](sheets/fees-and-costs/entry-and-exit-charges.md): Some funds take a charge when you put money in and when you take it out, on top of the yearly cost. Where to see them.
- [Payment for Order Flow](sheets/fees-and-costs/payment-for-order-flow.md): A market maker pays your broker for your order. Why that clashes with best execution, and how the EU banned it (fully from July 2026).
- [Your Total Cost Formula](sheets/fees-and-costs/your-total-cost-formula.md): Every cost line a European investor can pay, added up as one yearly sum, and the EU rule that firms must show it before and after.
